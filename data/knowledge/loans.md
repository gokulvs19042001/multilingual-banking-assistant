## What is a loan

A loan is money provided by a bank or other regulated lender to a borrower with an obligation to repay it according to agreed terms. The repayment normally includes the principal amount and applicable interest and charges. Before borrowing, customers should understand the total repayment obligation rather than looking only at the amount received.

## Interest rate and EMI

The interest rate determines the cost of borrowing, while an Equated Monthly Instalment, or EMI, is a periodic repayment amount used for many retail loans. The EMI may contain both principal and interest components. Customers should understand whether the interest rate is fixed, floating or otherwise structured according to the loan agreement.

## Loan eligibility and creditworthiness

Lenders assess a customer's ability and willingness to repay before approving credit. Factors can include income, existing indebtedness, repayment history and other information relevant to the lender's credit assessment. Customers should provide accurate information and avoid taking loans that exceed their repayment capacity.

## Digital lending

Digital lending allows customers to apply for or receive loans through digital platforms. Customers should verify the regulated lender, understand the role of any lending service provider and review the loan terms before accepting credit. Borrowers should be cautious about unverified loan applications that demand unnecessary permissions or sensitive information.

## Key facts before accepting a loan

A borrower should review the applicable interest rate, fees, repayment schedule, penalties and other important terms before accepting a loan. Digital loans may also provide a Key Fact Statement or equivalent disclosure containing important loan information. Customers should compare the overall cost of different borrowing options rather than choosing only on the basis of quick approval.

## Responsible borrowing

Borrowing should be based on a realistic assessment of income, expenses and existing debt. Missing repayments can create additional financial costs and may affect the borrower's credit history. Customers should contact the lender early if they anticipate repayment difficulties instead of ignoring the problem.
