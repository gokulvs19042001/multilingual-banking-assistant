## First complain to the bank

When a customer has a problem with a banking service, the first step is normally to raise the complaint with the concerned bank or regulated entity. The customer should use the bank's official complaint channel and retain the complaint or reference number. Clear transaction details and supporting documents can help the institution investigate the issue.

## Keep complaint evidence

Customers should preserve relevant records such as transaction references, account statements, emails, SMS messages, screenshots and complaint acknowledgements. These records can establish what happened and when the complaint was raised. They are particularly useful if the issue needs to be escalated later.

## RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme

The Reserve Bank's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme provides a grievance-redress mechanism for eligible complaints involving deficiency in service by covered regulated entities. The current RB-IOS 2026 came into effect on July 1, 2026 and replaced the 2021 scheme for new matters. It is designed to provide a cost-free and structured alternative grievance mechanism.

## When to approach the Ombudsman

A customer should normally first give the regulated entity an opportunity to resolve the complaint. If the complaint is not resolved satisfactorily or the customer does not receive a response within the applicable period, the customer may be able to approach the RBI Ombudsman subject to the scheme's maintainability requirements. Not every type of dispute is eligible, so customers should check the current scheme conditions.

## UPI and digital-payment complaints

For a failed, disputed or unauthorized digital-payment transaction, the customer should first raise the issue through the relevant bank, UPI application or payment-service grievance mechanism. The transaction reference number should be retained for tracking. If the regulated entity does not resolve an eligible service complaint, the customer may consider the applicable RBI grievance mechanism.

## Cybercrime complaints

Online financial fraud should be reported immediately through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or by calling 1930. Customers should also notify their bank or payment provider as soon as possible and request appropriate protective action. The cybercrime complaint and bank complaint should be retained as evidence for future follow-up.
